WorkGrid vs. HubSpot

HubSpot is the 800-pound gorilla of CRMs. Powerful marketing tools, massive ecosystem. But for small teams who just need to manage work — it's overkill in the worst way.

HubSpot

Marketing-first CRM

What they do well

  • Best-in-class marketing automation
  • Huge integration marketplace (1,000+)
  • Excellent content and SEO tools
  • Free CRM tier exists (limited)

Where they fall short

  • Free tier is a lead magnet, not usable — you hit paywalls instantly
  • No invoicing — you need a separate tool for that
  • No HR module at any price point
  • Starter plan: $20/mo per user, jumps to $500+/mo for real features
  • Steep learning curve — onboarding takes days to weeks
  • Task management is basic — not a real project tool
VS

WorkGrid

All-in-one workspace

Why we win here

  • CRM + tasks + invoicing + HR — HubSpot can't touch this combo
  • $0 during early access vs. HubSpot's $20–$500+/mo
  • Setup in 2 minutes, not 2 weeks
  • Role-based access that actually isolates modules

Honest caveat

  • No marketing automation (yet) — if email sequences are your core need, HubSpot still wins there
  • Smaller integration ecosystem (growing)
WorkGrid wins if: you need CRM + tasks + invoicing + HR without enterprise pricing

WorkGrid vs. Monday.com

Monday is a beautiful task management tool. The problem? It's ONLY a task management tool. The moment you need CRM, invoicing, or HR — you're shopping again.

Monday.com

Work OS platform

What they do well

  • Gorgeous, highly customizable board views
  • Great automations within boards
  • Strong team collaboration features
  • 200+ integrations available

Where they fall short

  • No real CRM/deal pipeline — their "CRM" template is just a board
  • Zero invoicing capabilities
  • No HR module — not even a basic one
  • $9+/user/mo and you still need 3+ other tools
  • Free tier limited to 2 seats and 3 boards
VS

WorkGrid

All-in-one workspace

Why we win here

  • Same board views PLUS deal pipeline, invoicing, and HR
  • One tool replaces Monday + Stripe + HR software + CRM
  • $0 vs. $9+/user — and you get 4x the functionality
  • No seat limits during early access

Honest caveat

  • Monday has more view types (Gantt, timeline) at launch
  • Monday's board automations are more mature
WorkGrid wins if: you need more than just task boards

WorkGrid vs. ClickUp

ClickUp tries to be everything. The result? A tool so packed with features that teams spend more time learning it than using it. And it still doesn't have invoicing or HR.

ClickUp

One app to rule them all?

What they do well

  • Insane number of features — docs, whiteboards, goals, time tracking
  • Very flexible view system (list, board, Gantt, timeline, calendar)
  • Generous free tier for personal use

Where they fall short

  • UI is overwhelming — new users get analysis paralysis
  • No invoicing — at any price tier
  • No HR module — at any price tier
  • "CRM" is just a custom view — no real pipeline logic
  • Onboarding takes days — teams resist adoption
  • Performance can lag with large workspaces
VS

WorkGrid

Focused all-in-one

Why we win here

  • Focused feature set — powerful but not paralyzing
  • Has invoicing and HR — the two things ClickUp is missing
  • Real deal pipeline, not a dressed-up task board
  • Teams actually adopt it — 2-minute onboarding

Honest caveat

  • No built-in docs, whiteboards, or time tracking (yet)
  • Fewer view types than ClickUp
WorkGrid wins if: you value adoption speed and having invoicing/HR built in

WorkGrid vs. Notion

Notion is an incredible knowledge base. Using it as a CRM is like using a spreadsheet as a database — technically possible, practically painful.

Notion

All-in-one workspace (for docs)

What they do well

  • Best-in-class docs and wikis
  • Incredibly flexible — build anything with databases
  • Generous free tier
  • Beautiful templates community

Where they fall short as a CRM

  • No real CRM — you're building one from databases (DIY)
  • No drag-and-drop pipeline — just table views
  • Zero invoicing — not even close
  • Zero HR — you're back to Google Sheets
  • No role-based access control
  • Sync issues with large teams
VS

WorkGrid

Purpose-built workspace

Why we win here

  • Real CRM with pipeline — not a DIY database project
  • Invoicing built in — no "connect Stripe to Notion" hacks
  • HR portal with actual role isolation
  • Kanban boards that work like Kanban, not filtered tables

Honest caveat

  • Notion is still better for docs/wikis — use it alongside WorkGrid
  • Less flexible for building custom database structures
WorkGrid wins if: you need actual business tools, not a blank canvas

WorkGrid vs. Salesforce

Salesforce is the enterprise standard. If you have a 200-person sales team and a six-figure CRM budget, it's great. For everyone else — it's enterprise overkill.

Salesforce

Enterprise CRM giant

What they do well

  • Unmatched enterprise CRM capabilities
  • Massive AppExchange ecosystem
  • Deep customization via Apex/Flows
  • Industry-specific solutions (healthcare, finance, etc.)

Where they fall short for small teams

  • $25–$300+/user/mo — budget-destroying for small teams
  • Requires certified admin to set up properly
  • No built-in task management — you need a separate tool
  • No invoicing or HR natively
  • Interface feels like a 2010 enterprise app
  • Onboarding takes weeks to months
VS

WorkGrid

The anti-enterprise CRM

Why we win here

  • $0 vs. $25–$300+/user — save thousands per year
  • No admin needed — anyone can set it up
  • Tasks + CRM + invoicing + HR in one — Salesforce needs 4+ tools for this
  • Modern UI your team will actually enjoy using

Honest caveat

  • Not built for 200+ person enterprise sales teams (yet)
  • No Apex-level customization or complex workflow automation
WorkGrid wins if: you're not enterprise and don't want to pay enterprise prices
